## Why this entry exists

Ledger 0026 showed that reading pro-league one scene at a time gave a wrong
model twice in one session. The same discipline applied to the bible: all
twenty documents, read in full, in layer order (Magic System → Vampirism →
Organizations → Characters → Timeline). Running notes were kept as the read
went; they are the catalogue at the end of this entry, verbatim.

## Finding 0 — two of my own ledgers were partial reads

**Ledger 0001's headline is false.** It said every fact-conflict came from the
v1→v2 revision pass and that the v1 documents are "internally and mutually
consistent". Three v1-vs-v1 conflicts say otherwise:

| v1 document | says | against |
|---|---|---|
| v1 Renaissance | *"Born 1770"* **and** *"1870: Lucynda Born into Crisis"*; first love dies *"1865"* | itself |
| v1 Modern | Cassandra died of a *"mysterious illness"* (OMC experimentation) | v1 profile, v1 tragedy, v1 vampirism: explosion, 1805 |
| v1 Modern | *"Humans cannot become vampires… kill… not infect them"* | v1 Renaissance: human mages *"identified and infected"* |

It also under-counted F1 (birth: ~1775 and 1870 missing) and F3 (patricide: 1923
missing), and described `summary_revision` as two phases when it has six.

**Ledger 0023 finding 3 was a false negative.** "The origin of human mages is not
written anywhere": it is written three times, and the three accounts disagree
(Mesopotamia ~3500 BCE / post-Veil awakening of conduit bloodlines ~500 CE /
generations of practice).

Both entries now carry dated corrections. **NAS-C9's evidence line ("v1 is
clean, the revision pass broke it") does not stand.** The v1→v2 pass did
introduce conflicts (ME3, RE3 below), but it is not the only source.

## Finding 1 — the conflicts cluster on five axes, and all five are the author's

Grouped from the catalogue. None is decidable from the documents; each needs
the author.

1. **Lysandra's transformation.** Consent agrees everywhere (voluntary). Three
   other things don't:
   - **Motive.** Protection: v1 + v2 profiles, OMC doc, v1 Modern,
     `summary_revision`, `Calude_v2`. Contributing to research: v1 tragedy,
     v2 vampirism.
   - **What she knew.** Ignorant: `Calude_v2`, v2 vampirism, v1 tragedy.
     *"Full knowledge of the consequences"*: OMC doc.
   - **The threat.** The OMC (v1 profile, v1 Modern, OMC doc) or vampire
     retaliation (`Calude_v2`).

   This is the moral centre. Ledger 0001 dissolved the consent question and
   never looked at these three.
2. **Orion's culpability** — five versions: unwitting (v1 tragedy, Lucas doc),
   accidental while curing her (v2 vampirism), accidental on other projects (v1
   profile), knowing architect of an OMC programme (OMC doc, v1 Renaissance:
   *"project head"*), leader of a deliberate weapon (short vampirism).
3. **Who knows, and where Orion's papers went** — released publicly in 1920
   (OMC doc, v1 Renaissance, v1 Modern, `summary_revision`), released 1945–50
   (`Calude_v2`, Custodes, vampirism docs, and a second stage in two of the
   1920 docs), **sealed** (v1 tragedy), **died with him** (v2 vampirism, Lucas
   doc). Lucas's whole plot — rediscovering forbidden knowledge — depends on the
   last two. This is KnowledgeScope, the thing NAS models.
4. **Human mages** — are victims turned or only killed (M-b); is extinction
   complete in WWI or near in WWII (M-h); do survivors exist today (Lucas
   "first in decades" vs *"surviving human mages"*); where did they come from
   (R1/ME1).
5. **Dates on one spine** — birth (1770 / 1763 / ~1775 / 1870), Cassandra's
   death (1805 / ~1810 / illness), transformation (1910 / ~1915 / 1907 / ~1815),
   patricide (1920 / 1923 / ~1825), vampire awakening (1897–1905 vs 1914+),
   Blood Tears (1943 / ~1945 / 1939–45).

## Finding 2 — one word means two things

**"Quarantine"** means (1) the magical world sealed off from humans so human
magic cannot develop (v2 vampirism, Lucas doc) and (2) the world's magical
communities embargoing *Europe* for contagion (Modern Era docs).
`summary_revision` uses both. The embargo itself is permanent in
`summary_revision` and lifted by 1957 in v1 Modern and `Calude_v2`. A term with
two referents is the query-divergence class (NAS-C13) at the level of a single
noun.

## Finding 3 — a false positive caught by the twentieth document

Mid-read I logged *Sterling vs Androxis* as two people (the Custodes doc names a
"Lieutenant Sterling Partnership" and then "Lieutenant Androxis"). `Calude_v2`
writes *"Lieutenant Androxis (Sterling)"*: one person. Same shape as ledger
0001's Q1: **the document that dissolves the apparent conflict was in the corpus
the whole time**, and only a complete read finds it. Recorded as CV1;
M-g withdrawn.

## What this settles

- A partial read of a hand-maintained corpus produces confident, wrong
  headlines. Three of my own (0001 headline, 0023 finding 3, the M-g entry
  here) in one corpus.
- The highest-value conflicts aren't dates. They are **motive, knowledge and
  culpability** — the axes NAS models as valence, KnowledgeScope and agency —
  and no document-diff finds them, because each document is internally
  plausible.
