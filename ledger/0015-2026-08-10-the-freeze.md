## The plan, and why executing it killed it

§16.5 read: *reorder the sections into the order the seed thread forces, and
switch every cross-reference to a slug anchor in the same pass so the
renumbering can never drift again.* Written in v0.14, right after ledger 0003,
as that entry's lesson applied rather than repeated.

Both halves fail on contact.

**Slug anchors do not survive renumbering, because they contain the number.**
`#76-composition-and-emergence` is what GitHub generates from `### 7.6
Composition and Emergence`, and it breaks the instant §7.6 becomes §9.2. The
proposed mitigation and the proposed hazard were the same operation.

**And the renumbering is the hazard.** Ledger 0003's citation drift — ten stale
references, silent through four version bumps *and* a five-judge adversarial
full read — was **caused by a renumbering.** §16.5 proposed doing it once more,
at a moment when the numbers are cited by fifteen ledger entries, SOFTWARE.md,
two project corpora and roughly two hundred places in NAS.md.

So the freeze became the opposite operation. **The fix for "renumbering breaks
citations" is not a better anchor. It is to stop renumbering.**

## What the freeze actually is

**1. Section numbers are permanent IDs.** §14.1 has said since v0.4 that IDs are
*stable, never reused or renumbered* — of register rules. It never occurred to
the document to apply that to itself. Now it does: a `§` number is an address,
frozen on assignment, never reordered even when the reading order argues for it.
New material takes the next free number or a **letter suffix** where it belongs
beside an existing section — §8.6b, which I had already done instinctively three
hours earlier without noticing I was inventing the rule.

**2. The reading order is declared rather than imposed** (§0.1) — an eight-row
path through frozen numbering. The largest divergence is real and now stated:
**§7 (the world) and §8's agent machinery come before §3 (observers)**, because
a story builds a world and its actors before there is anyone to hide things
from. The file is numbered the other way for historical reasons that are now
permanent. A reader follows a table; fifteen ledgers keep working. That is the
trade, made explicitly.

**3. The register is sorted** — 46 active rules alphabetical then numeric,
retired `WORLD-1` at the foot. It had drifted into insertion order (GRAPH-9
between GRAPH-4 and GRAPH-5; GRAPH-6/7/8 after VAL-4), which is tolerable in a
changelog and not in the one table whose entire job is being scanned.
Rule-set identity was diffed before and after: **no rule lost, none duplicated.**

**4. Every cross-reference verified.** Mechanical check across NAS.md,
SOFTWARE.md, `profiles/interactive.md` and `queries.md`: extract every heading,
extract every `§` reference, diff the sets. **One true dangle** — SOFTWARE.md
cited `§16.5` when §16's items were a bare numbered list with no headings. Fixed
by making the items addressable (§16.1–16.6) rather than by making the citation
vaguer, which is the direction the freeze should always resolve in.

Result: **zero dangling references** in the corpus.

## What is deliberately not frozen

**The models.** §14 keeps running; rules keep being revised by ledger evidence;
this document keeps changing. What froze is the **addressing scheme** — the
thing whose instability was silently corrupting citations, and the only thing a
"language freeze" should ever have meant here.

A freeze that stopped the models moving would have killed the evidence loop,
which is the one part of this system §17 believes has no precedent. Nothing that
can still be proven wrong got locked; the labels on the filing drawers did.

## The pattern this makes three of

Three times now a rule the document wrote about stories has turned out to apply
to the document: PATTERN-1 retired WORLD-1 from its own register (ledger 0011),
DRIFT-1 caught SOFTWARE.md running Wall 2 (ledger 0013), and now §14.1's ID rule
freezes the numbering that §14.1 lives inside.

That is not a coincidence worth admiring. It is the reason the reflexive checks
keep finding things: **this document is a hand-maintained corpus of interlocking
claims, which is exactly the artifact class NAS was built to instrument.** The
spec is its own smallest test case, and it has failed its own rules more often
than either project has.
