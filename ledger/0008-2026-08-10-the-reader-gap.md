# Ledger 0008 — the reader was undervalued, and one scene said how much

```yaml

subject: NAS.md — reader-facing machinery vs. canon-integrity machinery; the
         wants/fears orphan; the info-op / move convergence
```

## The observation

Raised by the author after VAL-4 had to be scoped twice — once for load-bearing
agents, once for canon-vs-reader-record: **"we might have undervalued one of the
key aspects. The reader."**

It is measurable, and the measurement is bad.

- **2 of 40+ register rules name the reader** (READER-1, READER-2). Three more
  are reader-adjacent (CONTRAST-1, SETUP-1, PUB-1). The remainder serve canon
  integrity.
- **The ratification pass found 15 defects (ledger 0005). Not one concerned
  whether a reader would feel anything.** Every one was internal consistency.

That is the ordinary failure of tooling — measuring what is easy to check rather
than what the thing is for — and §3 states outright that the reader is *"the
observer whose experience is the product."*

## The orphan

§3 declares, once: *"The reader's record additionally carries experiential
targets — `wants` and `fears`."*

**Nothing else in the document ever uses them.** No rule, no lint, no query, no
schema position. Declared and never connected — the same defect class caught
three times on the same day (`arc`, `internal_contradiction`, Oyo's debt
invariant), applied here to the object the system says is the point.

It survived twelve versions because it reads like a statement of values rather
than a field.

## The convergence

§3.1's information operations (v0.2) and §8.5's move vocabulary (v0.14) turn out
to be the same operations, derived twelve versions apart from opposite ends:

| §3.1 info op | §8.5 move |
|---|---|
| foreshadow | `open / imposed` |
| reveal | `close / bound` |
| mislead | `alter / redirect` |
| subvert | `close / foreclosed` + `open` |
| **reframe** | `alter / **reframe**` |

The last row is the evidence. The same word, the same meaning, arrived
independently in two vocabularies written a year apart in project time. Per the
house epistemology that is not tidying — it is the signal that both were
describing one shape.

**Implication:** the reader holds valences, and the info ops are moves on them.
`wants` and `fears` were valences all along; §3 named them and §7.6 built the
machinery, and neither section noticed the other.

## The test — and it changed the proposal

Rather than ratify, the unification was **run against the one rendered scene**
(`Chapters/ch03/s02.reader-state.md`), deriving the reader's valence state beat
by beat from `info_ops`, `emotional_temp` and `pacing_weight` — all authored
before the question existed. Invent nothing; report what is missing.

**Four of five beats derived cleanly with no new fields.** The mapping held on
first contact.

**Two things the test found that the argument had not:**

1. **`alter/escalate` is the load-bearing discovery.** Three of five beats are
   escalations — pressure rising on an open valence with *no new information* —
   and NAS has no way to say that. It is most of what a scene does between its
   reveals. `emotional_temp` was carrying it implicitly and nothing was reading
   it.

2. **One genuine gap, located precisely.** At b3, Tal saying her name opens
   *"why is she still holding?"* in the reader — and **no info op produced it.**
   It arises from a collapse elsewhere in the graph making an existing agent
   state suddenly unbearable. Derivable in principle; unrecordable in the scene
   interface as it stands.

So the addition is not "reader valences" — those derive. It is that **a collapse
can open a reader valence without an authored op**, and there is nowhere to say
so.

## Why this entry matters beyond its content

§16.3 claims proposals are cheaper to test against a rendered scene than against
another revision. This is the first time that claim was exercised, and **the test
changed the proposal**: it confirmed the convergence, killed the assumption that
reader machinery needed a new layer, and relocated the real gap from where the
argument had put it.

Cost: one file, no NAS.md edits, no new register rules.

## Not fixed

Nothing was ratified. The reader-side rules (open-valence-count as the sag
detector, `alter/escalate` as tension), the NAS-C12 restatement in reader terms,
and the collapse-opens-a-valence gap are all pending a **second scene** — one is
not a protocol.
