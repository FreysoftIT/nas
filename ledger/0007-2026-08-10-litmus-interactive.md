## The test

A second author's project, brought as a trial: an AI-rendered book adventure.
The model writes, the player decides. Cyberpunk; the player is a fixer; three
opening gigs, one chosen, and **the other two do not disappear — rival fixers
take them.** Stated theme: working with incomplete data.

Three questions were asked. All three produced findings, and one of them
produced a defect.

## 1. Does NAS help — yes, and one ratified rule turns out to be the architecture

Clean fits, none of them contrived: modality (§7.8) *is* the genre — every
piece of intel is a `saw`, and the fixer's job is triage; KnowledgeScope (§3)
makes information asymmetry the mechanic rather than the flavour; facets (§3.4)
are the relationship system; deltas-only (§4.1) is simply how the save file
would be built.

The load-bearing one is **PUB-1, fired every turn.** A novel meets its
publication boundary once. Here every rendered paragraph is instantly frozen
canon for that player, so the renderer *cannot* retcon — an external observer
already holds it. That makes GRAPH-2 and OBS-1 survival requirements rather
than hygiene, and it is a **harsher regime than a novel ever faces.** NAS had
the rule already, written for a different reason.

Corollary worth recording: **a model-driven work needs externalized state more
than a human-written one, not less.** The context window is a lossy cache of
the fold.

## 2. Does it need a fork — no. A profile, three flips, one forced decision

Three objects change *provenance*, not structure: the Cut becomes **derived per
session** (a GRAPH-4 query whose selection is the player's history), pillars
bind to **state conditions** rather than positions, and publication fires **per
turn**. Written up as `profiles/interactive.md`.

`mode: mixed` gets its first real use — hard on the world graph, soft on
emergent detail — which is exactly the per-scope definition ratified in #18 and
never exercised.

The forced decision was **modifiers** (§8.6), deferred twice in v0.14 and
ratified in v0.15 because an interactive work cannot start without them.

## 3. Does the cyberpunk premise beat the door — no, and the reason is the finding

**It is not a pillar.** §5 defines a pillar as a vivid fixed moment the author
is haunted by. *Three gigs, pick one, rivals take the rest* is a **mechanic** —
a designed space, not an image.

That is a finding rather than a criticism, and it opens a real question:
**interactive fiction may not originate from pillars at all.** A novelist is
haunted by a moment; a designer builds a space. Pillars may survive only as set
pieces the designer wants reachable on any path. Left open in the profile,
flagged as the one place it might yet prove to be a fork.

**The two seeds test orthogonal axes and neither wins.** The door tests the
**vertical** — one fact, a deep causal chain, a modality demotion, an
institution's two-century drift. The fixer premise tests the **horizontal** —
concurrent pursuits, competing voids, offscreen agent moves, information
asymmetry across many observers. The second reaches machinery the first
structurally cannot.

The premise's strongest element is its **theme**, not its structure: *working
with incomplete data* is §7.8 as a play loop, and a better fit than the door's
single rule, because Wren's incompleteness is one question asked once and a
fixer's is every scene, from every source, forever.

And a pillar was found hiding inside the mechanic: **meeting the consequence of
the gig you did not take** — the rival who took it, rich or dead or hunting you.
Generated by the author's own system rather than imported.

## The kicker — the test found a break v0.14 created

Working out where modifiers attach surfaced this: **v0.14 amended OBS-2 to take
the scene's declared field as an input while deferring the only mechanism by
which a field could alter an envelope.**

Under PATTERN-1's three-verdict test — ratified the same day — that is not
`weakens`. It is **`breaks`: an undeclared contract between a `gate | invariant`
rule and a layer that did not exist.** The rule was enforceable in name only for
one version.

It was found by a **second medium**, brought by a **second author**, asking
where a thing attaches. Not by reading, not by the five-judge adversarial panel,
not by the ratification pass that created it. That is the strongest evidence in
this ledger's history for the Field Atlas's Interlock claim that convergence
from a foreign starting point is evidence, and for §14's whole posture that
rules are revised by use rather than by inspection.

## Honest caveat

Nothing here has been built. The profile is a set of predictions about a medium
NAS has never run in, made by reasoning rather than by shipping — the same
status the Field Atlas's own entries carry before production contact. The
enforcement loop (preventing a model from rendering a fact that contradicts the
graph, in real time, under a regime where nothing can be taken back) is named
in the profile and solved nowhere.
