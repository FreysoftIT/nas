## The instrument

NAS-C9's protocol, in the register since v0.4: *corpus forensics — count
simplification-retcons per revision cycle.* ENGINE.md §1.4 (v0.5) gave it a
structural signature that judges nothing:

| flattening | signature |
|---|---|
| resolved over wounded | a defining wound closed in v2 that was open in v1 |
| victim over accomplice | causal agency in v1, only effect in v2 |
| significance over personhood | structural weight without interiority |

The corpus ships `Claude_v1_` and `Claude_v2_` of the same protagonist profile.
Matched pair, same character, same author, one revision cycle apart.

**Result: two signatures confirmed, one refuted.**

## Finding 1 — victim over accomplice, confirmed at heading level

The same span of her life, retitled between versions:

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| heading | **The Corruption Period** (1805-1880s) | **Growing Up and First Loss** (1798-1907) |

The body is the finding. **v1**:

> *"Her father would later use her as the genetic template for the vampire virus,
> creating beings driven by the same incompletion her mother had tried to cure."*

She is the causal root of vampirism. Every vampire in the world is derived from her
body. That is complicity without guilt — the deepest available position for a
protagonist, and the hardest to maintain, because everything downstream has to keep
referring to it.

**v2**, same span:

> *"During university, she formed her first romantic relationship with another
> anthro-dragon whose death in vampire attacks marked her first personal encounter
> with the growing crisis, **though she remained unaware of any family
> connection**."*

The genetic-template material is **gone from her life narrative**, and the closing
clause explicitly severs her from the causal chain for the period. She moves from
*cause of the catastrophe* to *person bereaved by it*.

**That is NAS-C9's prediction, exactly, in the direction it predicted** — and it is
legible from the section title alone, without reading either body.

## Finding 2 — significance over personhood, confirmed

v2's frame changed. Not a passage: the document.

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| opening | *Lysandra Sithernis (Lucynda) — Character Profile* | **`[CLASSIFIED DOCUMENT - MAXIMUM SECURITY CLEARANCE]`** |
| name | Lucynda — *"Known as"* | Lucynda — *"**Operational Name**"* |
| closing sections | Core Personality Traits · Current Status | Legacy and Future Implications · **Institutional Significance** · Research Implications · **Security Precedent** · *Conclusion: The Living Bridge* |

**The character profile became an intelligence dossier.** She is read as an asset
with an operational name, terminating in what she is a *precedent* for.

And the tell that names itself: v2 adds a section called **"Personal Life and
Humanizing Elements."** When personhood arrives as a section you append rather
than a property of everything else, it has become a feature of the file. NAS-C9
says *significance over personhood*; here the document supplies both words.

## Finding 3 — resolved over wounded: **refuted**

Predicted: v2 would close her defining wound, incompleteness. It does not.

v1 already has her complete — *"provided the natural completion that the virus
sought, making her the only 'successful' vampire with a completed philosopher's
stone."* The wound was never open in v1, so nothing was closed in v2.

**What actually changed is the category, not the state.** In v1 completion is a
tragedy: her mother died attempting it, and it is *why the virus sought her*. In v2
it appears under **"Unique Abilities and Capabilities"** as *"Completed
Philosopher's Stone Integration"*, with Vampire Interface Protocols and combat
applications.

> **Cost became asset.** The same fact, recategorised from the thing that ruined
> her family into the thing that makes her formidable.

That is a *fourth* signature and it was not in §1.4's table. It is subtler than
closing a wound and probably more common, because nothing is deleted — the
maintenance saving comes from no longer having to keep the fact *expensive*.

Added to §1.4 as `cost → capability`: *a fact that was load-bearing tragedy in v1
appearing under abilities in v2.*

## Finding 4 — §0's founding bug is still live, in the current document

Three lines of `Claude_v2_Lysandra Sithernis (Lucynda) - Complete Character
Profile`:

```
Age:  262 years old (as of 2025)     → born 1763
Born: 1770, Sithernis Estate         → born 1770
Early Life (1763-1798)               → born 1763
```

Which is §0's sentence, verbatim and unrepaired: *"a character is born in 1770 in
one paragraph and 1763 four paragraphs later — and her stated age is computed from
the wrong one."*

Corpus-wide there are **three** birth years:

| year | asserted in |
|---|---|
| **1770** | 7 files — both Vampirism docs, the Sithernis Family chronicle, v1 profile, v2 profile, two Timeline docs |
| **1763** | v2 profile only (the Age and the Early Life heading) |
| **~1775** | `Timeline/Renaissance_Enlightenment` |

And the version structure explains the shape. v1 is internally consistent (born
1770, age 255). v2 changed the birth year to 1763 and updated the age to 262 —
**but left `Born: 1770` in the Basic Information block, and never propagated to the
six other documents that cite 1770.**

So this is not one typo. It is **an un-propagated revision**, with the old value
still asserted in six places and the new value asserted twice inside the file that
changed it. Nothing caught it because, in §0's words, *"1770 is a string."*

## Assessment

**NAS-C9 has forensic confirmation in the corpus it was derived from.** The claim
predicted a direction of drift across revision cycles; the matched pair shows two
of its three named signatures, plus a fourth the claim did not name, and the
strongest one is visible from a heading rename.

Two things this changes.

**The signature is cheaper to detect than expected.** *The Corruption Period* →
*Growing Up and First Loss* encodes the entire flattening in a section title. A
check that diffs headings across versions and flags agency-language loss would have
caught the largest finding here for almost nothing.

**And the founding bug is a versioning failure, not a memory failure.** §0 frames it
as what happens when a writer holds too much. The forensics say something more
specific and more tractable: the writer *changed their mind correctly*, updated two
fields, and had no mechanism to propagate. That is not a lapse to be prevented by
attention. It is a fold that nobody computed.

*Instrument honesty:* three predictions, two confirmed, one refuted — and the
refuted one was replaced by a better finding that I had not predicted. Better than
this morning's 50% on ledger 0023, and the refutation is the reason to trust the
other two.
