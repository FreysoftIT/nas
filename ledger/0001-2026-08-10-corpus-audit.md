## Fact-conflicts (3)

### F1 · Lysandra's birth year — one node, two values, inside one document

| Document | Says |
|---|---|
| **v2 profile**, header | `Born: 1770` |
| **v2 profile**, next line | `Age: 262 years old (as of 2025)` → implies **1763** |
| **v2 profile**, §Early Life | `(1763-1798)` |
| v1 profile | `Born: 1770` · `Age: 255` · `Early Life (1770-1805)` — **coherent** |
| 5 other documents | 1770 |

2025 − 1770 = **255**, not 262. The v2 revision recomputed the age from 1763 and
shifted the Early Life range to match, then left `Born: 1770` untouched.
**1770 is canon across six documents; 1763 exists only inside the v2 profile,
twice.**

> **NAS verdict: impossible to represent.** Birth year is one node; age is a
> projection (§7.2). The "Age: 262" line is not a second error — it is a derived
> value that silently picked a parent that does not exist. *GRAPH-2.*

### F2 · The transformation date — three values

**1910** in eight places (v1 profile, v1 family tragedy, v2 vampirism,
`summary_revision`, OMC chronicle, v1 Modern Era, v1 Renaissance ×2) · **~1915**
in `Calude_v2` Modern Era · **1907** implied by the v2 profile's section
boundary `Transformation and Discovery (1907-1923)`.

### F3 · The patricide — a 95-year error

**1920** in six places · **~1920** in `Calude_v2` · and **`The Patricide
(~1825)`** in the v2 Renaissance timeline — a document whose own stated range is
*~1500-1800*, so the event is dated both 95 years early **and outside its
container**. The v1 edition of that same file reads `1920: Discovery of OMC
crimes` — correct.

> **NAS verdict: three gates.** GRAPH-1 (a `~1825` event cannot sit in a
> 1500–1800 container without a layer/interval violation), TIME-1 (the interval
> is malformed against its parent), and OBS-3 (the v2 edit was a retcon whose
> cone was never walked).

## Query-divergence (2) — the ones that matter

### Q1 · The transformation's agency — **the false positive**

Catalogued for months as *"father transformed her for protection" (profile) vs
"Voluntary Transformation" (vampirism doc) — agency/causal conflict.*

**There is no conflict.** `summary_revision` states all three facts in a single
block:

> **The Voluntary Transformation (1910):** *Protective Intent: **Orion
> transforming Lysandra** using refined virus strain … Family Love: Decision
> made from genuine desire to protect loved ones*

And the v1 family tragedy spells out the mechanism: she *"insisted on becoming a
test subject… volunteered repeatedly until Orion"* relented; *"the
transformation was entirely willing on Lysandra's part."*

**She volunteered. He administered. The purpose was protection.** The profile
answers *what was done and by whom*; the timeline answers *did she consent*.
Two questions, two selections, one consistent world.

> **This is the entry's most valuable finding.** The author had confirmed
> *voluntary* as canon and marked the profile line as drift to be corrected.
> **That correction would have deleted a true fact** — the father's agency,
> which is what makes the complicity load-bearing. NAS-C13 predicts
> non-fact-conflicts are *"invisible to document-diffing and unfixable by
> proofreading."* Here proofreading would have been actively destructive.
>
> *GRAPH-4* prevents it: had either projection recorded its query, the
> divergence would have been visible as a difference in *selection*, not in
> *fact*.

### Q2 · Two incompatible decompositions of the Modern Era

`Calude_v2` carves 1800–present into **three phases** by crisis arc (Vampire
Crisis ~1880-1957 · Revenge and Chaos ~1910-1945 · Political Transformation
~1945-1957). `summary_revision` carves it into **two** by origin (Genesis of
Horror 1800-1880 · Vampire Genesis Arc 1880-1920).

Neither is wrong. They answer *how did the crisis unfold* and *how did the
horror originate* — different questions, different time anchors. **But the
divergence conceals F2 inside it** (~1915 vs 1910), which is the exact reason
query-divergence is dangerous rather than merely noisy: it camouflages real
conflicts as stylistic difference.

## NAS-C9 — and the pattern nobody was looking for

**Every fact-conflict and the malformed value were introduced by the v1→v2
revision pass. All five v1 documents involved are internally and mutually
consistent.**

| Bug | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| birth year / age | ✅ 1770 / 255 | ❌ 1770 + 262 + 1763-1798 |
| transformation date | ✅ 1910 | ❌ 1907 (profile) · ~1915 (`Calude_v2`) |
| patricide | ✅ 1920 | ❌ ~1825 |
| Roman period | ✅ ~509 BCE – 476 CE | ❌ 509–476 BCE |

And the v2 profile is **internally coherent on a different timeline** — its four
life-stage boundaries were all rescaled together (1763-1798, 1798-1907,
1907-1923, 1923-present). Proofreading *within* the document finds nothing
wrong. Only a cross-document fold exposes it, which is precisely the operation
no human performs on twenty files.

**Direction: confirms**, with the standing caveat (n=1, self-reported). The
revision cycle is where the corpus broke, exactly as the flattening claim
predicts — and the drift is *systematic*, not sloppy, which is what makes it
survive review.

*Context, recorded neutrally:* the `Claude_v1_` / `Claude_v2_` filename prefixes
indicate these documents were generated and revised with model assistance. The
v2 pass regenerated rather than diffed, and produced four regressions across
four documents while leaving each one internally plausible. That is not an
argument against the method; **it is the strongest possible argument for
GRAPH-2** — a regenerated projection has no obligation to agree with the graph
it came from, and nothing in a folder of docx can enforce one.
