## The instrument

ENGINE.md §6.3 (v0.6) made the tool AI-less on principle: every help surface is
a **named, deterministic query** over a derived index, never a model call. It
named a candidate store — the Postgres dialect, embedded as PGlite — and one
test: rewrite `queries.md`'s seven hand-computed views as SQL, run them on the
corpus, diff.

`spikes/queries-test/`: `schema.sql` (the index), `load.mjs` (frontmatter →
rows, no model, one strict story-time parser), `queries.mjs` (the seven views
plus one diagnostic), `run.mjs` (writes `/out/queries-test.md`, gitignored —
GRAPH-9). Rebuilt from the files on every run; the store owns nothing (§5).

**Result: all seven views reproduce as SQL, each in under 5 ms. The hand
versions diverge from the corpus in fifteen places, silently — and none of the
fifteen is a case where the hand computation knew something the query could
not.**

## Finding 0 — the corpus had never been parsed

The loader's first run failed on `Pillars/pillar_01.md`: five postcondition
lines written `- agent: x, carries: y` are invalid YAML. 26 of 26 frontmatter
blocks parse after wrapping them in braces (repaired in place, noted in the
file, content unchanged).

The first object in either project to complete a lifecycle (`status:
rendered`) was not machine-readable. Every check that ever "ran" on it ran in
someone's head — which is §1.2's point, found at the byte level.

## Finding 1 — a state the spec names but never defines

NAS §8.5: pursuit is `held | pursued | closed`. `closed` is set by a close move.
**Nothing says what makes a pursuit `pursued`.** The spike had to choose — *≥1
attempt by the owner before the anchor* — and view 1's two disagreements turn
on that choice. The hand-computer chose too, silently, and differently. A
definition a machine has to invent is a hole in the language, not a detail.

## Finding 2 — authored snapshots disagree with the fold

Diagnostic view 8 compares every `entry_state` trust value (an authored
snapshot — SCENE-3 forbids them as truth) with the fold of `exit_deltas`:

| edge | scenes | agree |
|---|---|---|
| marek → kes | 6 | 6 |
| marek → oyo | 2 | 2 |
| oyo → marek | 1 | 1 |
| **kes → marek** | 5 | **1** — ch01 0.0 vs 0.3, ch03 0.1 vs 0.2, ch05 −0.4 vs −0.3, ch06 −0.4 vs −0.3 |

The consequence is concrete. `pillar_01` says precondition 3 (`kes→marek ≤
−0.4`) was **paid at ch03**. By the snapshots, yes: 0.1 − 0.5. **By the fold,
no:** ch03 exits at −0.3, and the edge first reaches ≤ −0.4 at **ch06** (−0.6).
PILLAR-1 still holds — both are before the pillar — but the recorded payment
site depends on which of two sources you believe, and they are the two NAS says
cannot both be authoritative. ch01's snapshot looks like it was written in
telling order (position 1) though ch01 sits *after* ch02 in chronology — the
two-fold rule, broken by hand in the one place it was never checked.

Not fixed here. Which number is the story's is the author's call.

## Finding 3 — the shooting is not in the graph

`queries.md` view 2 ends: *"The chain `Marek wins → Kes is trapped → Kes shoots
→ Oyo must save him` is **fully derivable from this graph**."* Three links are.
**`Kes shoots` is not**: Cut position 7 is `scene: null`, and no scene records
Kes's attempt or the move it makes. The link lives in a comment in `Cut.md` and
in the hand view's prose — so the claim of derivability was itself made by hand.

The fix is NAS's own: an offstage event is an attempt no scene has witnessed,
which §8.5 already allows as a **cloud**. It needs a place to be declared.

## Finding 4 — the query finds the scene nobody wrote

Current unstaged collisions (view 4):

- **Kes** — `facet_the_asset` (to Oyo) vs `facet_the_protege` (to Marek): Oyo and Marek have never been in a scene with her.
- **Marek** — `facet_the_mentor` (to Kes) vs `facet_the_operator` (to Oyo): same three people, same absence.

One gap, seen from two agents: **Marek, Kes and Oyo have never shared a room.**
§3.4 says facet collision is a scene generator; the query generated this one
in 4 ms, and the hand view, frozen a day early, could not.

## Finding 5 — VAL-4 on Kes

`val_kes_proof` forecloses `val_kes_out`; nothing forecloses back. Kes has **no
mutually-foreclosing pair**, and she is load-bearing (three `pillar_01`
preconditions run through her). By the letter of VAL-4 she fails. The hand
lints never ran VAL-4 on her — the fix note in `oyo.md` shows it was run agent by
agent, and she was not one of the agents.

Whether one-way foreclosure is tension enough is a real question for the rule,
and possibly the answer is yes. But it had not been asked.

## Structural tier — the first rule the project actually enforces

`structural` means "impossible by construction", in a project with no
construction (§1.2: 20 rules). The spike tested one:

- **TIME-2 (bilocation)** as an `EXCLUDE USING gist (entity WITH =, span WITH &&)`
  constraint. The corpus loads clean. A deliberately bilocated row (Marek, during
  ch07) is **rejected by the database**. First `structural` rule in either
  project enforced by something other than a comment.
- **GRAPH-5** (frozen edge vocabulary) as an `enum`; **§8.5's frozen move types**
  as a `CHECK`. Both hold on the corpus.

## What this settles

- **ENGINE.md §6.3's store: adopted** — Postgres dialect, embedded (PGlite),
  derived and rebuildable, never the truth. Load 1.5 s (engine start-up
  dominates); every query ≤ 5 ms on seven scenes.
- **The AI-less claim holds on this corpus.** Every view the author relied on for
  "help" is a deterministic query. Nothing needed judgment the files didn't
  already carry — and where the hand views *did* use judgment, it was the
  judgment that went stale.
- **`queries.md` is a GRAPH-2 violation** and should stop being hand-maintained.
  Its prose — *read it once and the plot is visible* — is commentary worth
  keeping; its tables should be generated. Not changed here: the author decides.
