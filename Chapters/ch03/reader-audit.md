## b3 — a foreshadow that reads as texture

The load-bearing choice in the scene, and the one the trajectory is built around.

The reader sees the whole exchange: Kes in the doorway, jacket on, weight even.
Three lines. *"Give me ten minutes." "It's—" "Ten minutes, Kes."* Then a jacket
and a door.

**It is declared as a `foreshadow` (∅ → hinted), not a `reveal`** — the reader
receives the event and cannot weigh it. Nothing is hidden and nothing is
explained. The exit state records `rv_who_was_at_the_door` and
`rv_what_did_she_say` as **open**, which is the honest reading: they now hold a
question they don't know is a question.

`ch07.s01` b3 is the `reveal` that decodes it — *"I'm past the rate," and he had
been looking at a schedule* — and the two words he actually said.

**Which makes this the project's first real test of the reread model (§3.3).**
On a first read b3 is texture: a colleague interrupts, gets deferred, leaves.
On a reread it is the whole book, and the reader's earlier record — *nothing
happened there* — is the thing they get to watch be wrong. That gap is
late-record vs early-record for the same observer, which is exactly what §3.3
ratified rereader irony to be, and nothing in the project had exercised it.

## The thing the scene is built to avoid

**Marek is not cruel here, and the machinery is what keeps him from being.**

The temptation in this scene is to have him brush her off — to make the moment
legible by making him briefly a bad man. The graph forbids it, and usefully:

- `under_scrutiny: "takes the harder contract"` fires, so he is *concentrating*,
  not dismissing
- his epistemic modifier says he reads `fact_burn_rate` as `is` and she reads it
  as `must`, so **he does not know they are looking at different objects**
- his invariant — *never sends a runner somewhere he wouldn't go* — is intact in
  his own mind, because he has not yet been asked a question he'd have to break
  it to answer

So what the reader watches is a man being *slightly rude while busy*, which is
the most ordinary thing in the scene and is the hinge of the plot. **The
foreclosure fires as a side effect of competence.** No villainy was available:
`val_marek_standing ──forecloses──> val_kes_out` is a graph edge, and graph edges
do not require anyone to intend anything.

## What this scene closed

1. **`pillar_01` precondition 1** — the binding the pillar's postconditions had
   assumed since it was written. Five unpaid → **four**.
2. **ch07's orphaned payoff** — `setup_one_more` had a payoff and no setup.
   §8.4's payoffs-without-setups query had exactly one hit; it now has none.
3. **The plot's central foreclosure**, which had been a line in `queries.md` and
   is now an event with a scene reference.

Three obligations, none of them invented for this scene, all of them discharged
by the same eight hundred words.
