# Worked example — *Pro League* · the horizontal axis

**Companion to the door seed (NAS §0.1), not a replacement.** NAS.md stays
single-seeded on purpose: it builds one story from fourteen words so a reader
can follow every derivation, and adding a second seed to that document would
re-create the exact defect ledger 0004 was opened to fix.

This example exists because the door can only demonstrate one axis.

| | The door (§0.1) | Pro League (here) |
|---|---|---|
| **Axis** | **vertical** — one fact, a deep causal chain, a modality demotion, an institution's two-century drift | **horizontal** — three agents, concurrent pursuits, competing voids, cross-agent foreclosure |
| **Real agents** | one (Wren; Tal is offstage, Oris is a memory) | three, all present, all acting |
| **Demonstrates** | collapse, layer choice, `must`/`saw`, retcon cones | conflict-as-structure, relationship asymmetry, modifier stacks, valence succession |
| **Best for** | learning the system | stress-testing it |

## The pillar

> **The fixer made the pro league. Now he is in the street, riddled with
> gunshot wounds, put there by his top edgerunner — the one he mentored. And
> his staunchest rival is the one trying to save his sorry skin.**

---

## What is already canon

Before any machinery — read it again and count what it has committed you to.
The door's seed had four commitments. This one has six, and three of them are
relationships that were inverted before the sentence ended.

- **"made the pro league"** — a valence **bound**. He got the thing. And the
  next clause is catastrophe, which is §7.7's ladder arriving as violence
  instead of as sag (NAS-C12).
- **"managed to"** — it cost him. There were attempts, they were expensive, and
  they are the reason for everything downstream.
- **"the one he mentored"** — **he made the person who shot him.** The causal
  edge runs *through* the victim; GRAPH-3 is satisfied by construction rather
  than by discipline.
- **"staunchest rival"** — long, adversarial, with history. Not a stranger, and
  therefore not a coincidence.
- **"trying to"** — ongoing, outcome unknown. The same per-second re-decision
  the door's *holds* commits you to, transferred to someone with no obvious
  reason to keep deciding it.
- **"his sorry skin"** — narration in his own register, contemptuous of
  himself. That is a POV commitment and a facet: how he presents *to himself*
  (§3.4), which the reader now has and the other two agents do not.

**What is missing:** why she shot him. Not undecided — absent. Same void as the
door's missing reason, and it recruits the same way.

## The finding this example produced

Building the three agents surfaced something ratified in v0.14 and never
noticed, because the door has only one agent to notice it with:

> **`forecloses` works across agents, and cross-agent foreclosure is the
> structural definition of conflict.**

§7.6 defines `forecloses: [valence_ids]` as "binding this one closes those."
Nothing in it says *same agent*. So:

- **Marek** binds `val_marek_standing` (make the league) — which forecloses
  **Kes's** `val_kes_out`, because league work is what keeps her running.
- **Kes** acting on that forecloses **Oyo's** `val_oyo_win`, because a rival
  shot dead by his own runner is a rival Oyo never beats.

Three agents, one chain, and **nobody plotted it.** The shooting and the rescue
are both derivable from the valence graph — which is what §7.6 claims
relationships and plots become once valence is unified ("predicted chemistry
rather than authored decoration"), demonstrated on the first structure with
enough agents to test it.

Within one agent, foreclosure is internal contradiction (§8.1, derived).
**Across agents, the same edge is plot.** One relation, two readings by
altitude — the same shape the composition ladder shows everywhere else.
