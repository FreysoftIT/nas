# Reader state across `ch03.s02` — a test, not a proposal

> ⚠ **Superseded framing — see `reader-model-test.md`.** This file derives
> *"the reader's valence state"* as though it were a fact. **It is not.** The
> reader is unmodellable — baggage, reading skill, bias, attention — and a
> system claiming to know what they feel is lying in a way that eventually
> costs the author something.
>
> What survives here is the **first row** of the correct three-way split: what
> the reader has been *told*, derivable from the info-op stream. What this file
> calls the reader's state is really **the author's intent**, reverse-engineered
> after the fact — which is why the framing only broke once a second scene
> declared its intent *before* the prose.
>
> Read it for the op/move mapping, which held. Read `reader-model-test.md` for
> what the object actually is.

**Question:** does the reader's experience of this scene fall out of data that
already exists, read as valences (§7.6) and moves (§8.5)? Or does it need
machinery NAS doesn't have?

**Method:** take the scene's declared `info_ops` and `beats` — both authored
before this file existed, neither written with this question in mind — and try
to derive the reader's valence state at each beat. Invent nothing. Where
something is missing, say so.

*query: {selection: reader valences, scope: reader, anchor: ch03.s02 by beat, audience: writer}*

## Findings

**1. It works, and it needed no new fields.** Every row above is derived from
`info_ops`, `emotional_temp` and `pacing_weight` — all authored before this test
existed. The mapping in the proposal held on first contact:

- `foreshadow` → `open/imposed` (b4)
- `reveal` → `close/bound` (b3)
- rising tone across beats with no information delivered → `alter/escalate` (b1, b2, b5)

**2. `alter/escalate` is the load-bearing discovery.** Three of five beats are
escalations — pressure rising on an already-open valence with no new information.
**NAS currently has no way to say that**, and it is most of what a scene does
between its reveals. `emotional_temp` was carrying it implicitly and nothing was
reading it.

**3. The scene is well-formed by a measure that did not exist this morning.**
Enters with 2 open, binds 1, opens 2, exits with 3. The reader never holds
nothing, and the binding at b3 costs more than it settles — which is why the
scene does not deflate at its own reveal.

**4. b3 opens a valence the scene never declared, and this is the one real gap.**
When Tal says her name, the reader immediately holds *why is she still holding?*
— and no `info_op` produced it. It arises from a **collapse elsewhere in the
graph** (`fact_tal_lucidity` bound) rendering an existing agent state suddenly
unbearable.

That is derivable in principle — a reader valence opened by a world collapse
rather than by an authored op — but nothing in the scene interface records it,
so the writer could not have known to check for it. **This is the one place the
test found something missing rather than something unconnected.**

---

## Verdict

**The unification is real for four of five beats and needs one addition, not a
layer.** The addition is not "reader valences" — those derive cleanly — it is
that **a collapse can open a reader valence without an info op**, and the
interface has nowhere to say so.

Two candidate shapes, neither adopted here:

- extend `info_ops` with an implicit-open kind, authored where the writer
  anticipates it
- or treat it as fully derived: any collapse that changes what an open agent
  state *means* to the reader opens a valence, computed rather than declared

The second is truer and harder. Both are cheaper to decide against a second
scene than against this paragraph.

**What this test cost:** one file, no changes to NAS.md, no new register rules.
**What it bought:** the info-op/move convergence confirmed on real data, one
genuine gap located precisely, and `alter/escalate` identified as the missing
representation for the thing scenes spend most of their length doing.

*Per §16.3: proposals are cheaper to test against a rendered scene than against
another revision. This is that, run once, and it changed the proposal.*
