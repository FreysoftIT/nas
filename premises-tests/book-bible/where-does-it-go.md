## 1. The two problems are one problem

**Observed: the corpus is a history.** Every document terminates at the present.
Timeline runs `3000 BCE → Roman → Medieval → Renaissance → Modern (1800–Present)`.
The Organizations chronicles end in collapse and aftermath. The character profiles
end in *Current Status* and *Current Operational Status*.

**Observed: the second lead is defined by function, not want.** Counting the
vocabulary of his own headings and body against Lysandra's:

| | function-words | interior-words |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lucas / Raffaele (v1) | **22** | **1** |
| Lysandra (v2) | 30 | 3 |

*function = significance · importance · potential · capability · asset · strategic ·
value.  interior = wants · fears · regret · guilt · ashamed · longing.*

His headings: *Magical Significance and Historical Importance* · *Strategic
Importance* · *Alchemical Potential* · *Cultural and Linguistic Assets* · *Bridge
Building Capabilities* · *Research Revolution Catalyst* · *Irresistible Target
Status* · *Why Raffaele Is Both Hope and Prey*.

**And all three of his stated motivations are instrumental** — quoted in full,
because this is the load-bearing evidence:

> *"Driven by desire to heal and innovate in medicine creation, making him
> **naturally aligned with** finding cures…"*
> *"…the perfect storm of knowledge, motivation, and capability **needed to finally
> solve** the vampire crisis."*
> *"…gives him both the motivation and **the moral authority to pursue** dangerous
> research."*

**The diagnosis, in NAS terms: he has no want that can be foreclosed.** §8 defines
conflict structurally as cross-agent foreclosure — A's pursuit closing B's. A want
that is *"naturally aligned"* with the mission collides with nothing and costs
nothing, so it generates no conflict and needs no interior.

**The connection.** Characters acquire wants because something in the future
demands payment. **A corpus with no forward moment never asks anyone to want
anything at cost**, so its people are written as functions. That is not thin
writing; it is the structural consequence of a history book.

*Evidence that the causation runs this way:* in `pillar_01`, one declared future
moment produced a six-row precondition table, six scenes were written to pay it,
and **three agents acquired valences with foreclosures that were never authored as
character work.** `pillar_01.md` records that Oyo's contaminated-win structure
"fell out of fixing a VAL-4 lint" — the depth was a by-product of the forward
structure.

**Suggestion: do not write Lucas. Declare the pillar and read what it demands of
him.** A precondition arrives as a debt, and a debt is a want with a price
attached.

## 2. Two structures the bible already has, which any pillar will sit on

Stated so they are not re-derived, and because both were checked before being
listed.

**The completion knot — already written, verbatim:**

> *"Lysandra's completed philosopher's stone makes her simultaneously the solution
> other vampires seek and the weapon most capable of stopping those who cannot be
> helped."*

The cure walks around inside a person the disease is built to hunt. *Note the
framing:* the line appears as a **capability**, under abilities. What it is
structurally is a standing danger to her, and nothing in the corpus treats it as
one. **Suggestion, not correction** — you may have framed it as power on purpose.

**The research paradox — already written, with its costs named:**

> Curing vampirism *"requires experimentation that closely resembles Orion's
> original research methodology."*

And the taboo blocking it has **four documented enforcement mechanisms**:
prohibition, institutional terror, social ostracism and career destruction,
political persecution. **This is the only forward tension in the corpus whose cost
is already fully specified.**

### Candidate B, sketched — because its costs are already paid for

Offered as a worked example of what the derivation *looks like*, not as a
recommendation. If B were declared:

```yaml

  - taboo exists and is enforced                    # ✅ PAID — four mechanisms
  - the cure requires Orion's method                # ✅ PAID — stated verbatim

  - someone stands to lose everything by proceeding # ⚠ UNPAID — see below
```

**Three unpaid, and each one is a scene that would have to exist.** That is the
mechanism working: the table names debts without writing them.

**What Lucas would have to want under B**, stated as a constraint rather than a
trait: *something that the four enforcement mechanisms can take from him.* Today
he holds **moral authority** — the text says so — which is the opposite of
exposure. A want that institutions can foreclose is a want they can price.

**The third unpaid item is the sharpest and it is checkable:** the research taboo
is enforced by *four mechanisms* and **no named agent**. Someone benefits from the
cure staying unstudiable. Under §8 that person has a valence, and it forecloses
Lucas's. *No such character exists in the corpus* — verified across all extracted
documents.

## 4. The limit — stated because the tool will have it too

**The real pillar may not be on this list, and the method cannot tell you.**

These four were extracted from text. `pro-league`'s pillar was a sentence the
author already carried — *"the fixer made the pro league; now he is in the street,
put there by his top edgerunner, and his staunchest rival is trying to save his
sorry skin."* No document contained it. No analysis would have produced it.

**A generator can surface what a corpus implies. It cannot surface what haunts the
writer.** If one of these four is not the moment you actually see, the correct
output of this run is *"none of these"* — and that is a result, not a failure.

## 5. What is being asked of the writer

Exactly one decision, and nothing here presumes it:

> **Which moment do you see?** One of the four, or one you already carry.

Everything downstream — the precondition table, what Lucas must want, which scenes
have to exist, whether the taboo needs a face — derives from that answer and
cannot be derived without it.
